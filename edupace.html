TU Delft Minor Biomedical Engineering · 2025

EduPace is a blended software + hardware simulator that trains nurses and biomedical engineering students to configure a temporary external single-chamber pacemaker. The platform pairs a real-time ECG engine with a tactile control unit, so learners can practice pacing, sensing, and threshold tuning in a setting that mirrors the clinical workflow.

Project overview

Developed in the TU Delft Minor Biomedical Engineering, EduPace recreates the behavior of the Medtronic 53401 Temporary External Pacemaker for realistic training scenarios. Learners can rehearse device configuration, interpretation, and patient-response adjustments before entering the clinic. The simulator targets bradycardia caused by AV blocks, where dependable ventricular pacing is essential to restore adequate cardiac output and prevent hemodynamic collapse.

Clinical relevance

Models first-, second-, and third-degree AV blocks with realistic pacing response.

Why temporary pacing training matters

Temporary external pacemakers stabilize patients with bradycardia caused by sinoatrial-node dysfunction or atrioventricular (AV) blocks. Because these devices are used infrequently on the ward, many nurses do not practice the setup and adjustments needed to respond quickly in emergencies. EduPace closes that gap with a realistic, repeatable learning environment that mirrors real-world pacing workflows.

  • Focuses on ventricular pacing to manage AV conduction failures.
  • Reinforces recognition of ECG changes across AV block severity.
  • Simulates capture thresholds and sensing behavior for safer pacing.
  • Provides instructor-ready scenarios for rapid onboarding and refreshers.

System components

EduPace is built around two primary components: a software simulator that models ECG response in real time, and a tactile hardware console that mirrors hospital pacemakers. Together they form a blended training loop for pacing, sensing, and threshold tuning.

Software

ECG modeling & interactive pacing lab

The ECG engine responds to three adjustable parameters (rate, output, and sensitivity) and supports four training rhythms: NSR, 3rd degree AV block, 2nd degree AV block (Mobitz II), and slow conduction. Learners can quickly compare pacing strategies and see how the waveform shifts with each adjustment.

  • Pathology-specific traces tuned for pacing response.
  • Waveform segments react dynamically to output and sensitivity changes.
  • Scenario swapping for fast instructor-led drills.
  • Instant feedback on pacing spikes and capture.

Electron-ready training app

The simulator was built as a responsive web app, packaged into an Electron desktop application, and distributed through GitHub releases for offline lab setups.

  • Dashboard for scenario selection and pacing readiness checks.
  • Training tab with ECG playback, step-by-step guidance, and outcomes.
  • Settings pane for device pairing, patient presets, and session exports.
Hardware

Arduino-based control unit

The physical trainer uses an Arduino GIGA R1 with a touchscreen shield, rotary encoders, and indicator LEDs that match the Medtronic 53401 layout. The enclosure was custom-modeled for comfortable, one-handed pacing adjustments in a training room.

  • Rotary encoders emulate click-by-click rate, output, and sensitivity tuning.
  • 3D-printed casing refined for lightweight, clinical-style handling.
  • USB-C serial link keeps hardware and software synchronized.
  • Modular internals for quick maintenance and upgrades.

Design highlights

01

Hardware choices

Arduino GIGA R1 was selected for the display shield compatibility and higher graphics performance compared to the earlier Arduino Mega build.

02

Display upgrade

The 800×480 touchscreen replaces 7-segment displays and reproduces labels, indicators, and lock/power states from the Medtronic 53401.

03

Rotary encoders

Incremental encoders emulate the click-by-click adjustment of rate, output, and sensitivity settings with directional logic.

04

Ergonomic enclosure

The 3D-printed casing was refined to be thin, lightweight, and comfortable for one-handed use in training sessions.

Development process

EduPace progressed through rapid prototyping, clinical feedback sessions, and iterative refinements to the hardware, software, and enclosure. The team moved from an Arduino Mega-based prototype to the Arduino GIGA R1 platform, redesigned the enclosure multiple times for better ergonomics, and refined the ECG modeling to align with clinical pacing behavior and expected capture thresholds.

The team validated the final hardware in live training sessions and documented the electronics, enclosure, and pacing engine for future cohorts.
